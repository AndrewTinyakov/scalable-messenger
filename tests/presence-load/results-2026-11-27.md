The current smoke check passed. Both legacy smoke attempts failed with 61 of 62 intended sockets ready, then reached 64 of 64 in the stranger-check window. The final sidebar-only actor reconnected 2.5 seconds before the smoke cutoff. A one-socket probe on the same account reproduced the timing. The socket stayed open with zero of 51 states at 2.5 seconds and became ready after the first sidebar poll at 3.29 seconds. This fit the legacy five-second poll and the identical 51-state deficit in both smoke reports, so the full run proceeded. Both protocols denied the stranger watch.

## Method

Both Compose projects ran on the same Apple M3 Pro with 11 logical CPUs and 36 GiB RAM. OrbStack had 11 CPUs and 15.7 GiB RAM. The driver used Node 24.21.0. Each project had two gateways, two APIs and Redis 7.4.11 with three primaries and three replicas. The full run took 1 hour 52 minutes. For each scenario the driver ran legacy, then current, one at a time, with a 60-second pause after each step.

The legacy gateway image was tagged `e56bc28`. The current gateway was rebuilt after `f891c89`. This run used the driver at `e7ee76b`, which only changed browser-client files after the gateway rebuild. The stacks reused their build-specific API images (`6d8507362a1b` and `65362849d9e5`, respectively).

The two stacks had the same seeded plan of 16,384 users, 414,547 direct chats and 256 groups, with seed 1118 and one device per user. The driver fetched up to 150 tickets/s before timing and attempted up to 100 new sockets/s during ramp-up. Timed windows exercised presence without sending chat messages. The driver used the same watch sets across the two protocols and counted application frames received at the driver. Prometheus rates are window means for each stack. Redis commands include shared room and session traffic. Delivery p99 is the histogram upper bound for on-time deliveries. Delivery fractions use armed watcher observations. The driver stayed below its 50 ms event-loop p99 cutoff in all 51 windows.

Every socket was ready at the end of all six quiet windows. At 3,500 sockets, mean gateway CPU fell from 0.186 to 0.027 cores, while gateway RSS rose from 431 to 532 MiB. API gRPC fell from 231.1 to 2.1 calls/s. The current build made zero `PresenceVisibility` and `OnlineCountMembers` calls in that window. Session validation accounted for about 2.0 calls/s.

At 3,500 sockets with status changes, the legacy build sent 1,392.8 frames/s and the current build sent 212.4. Hot-subject offline p99 fell from 8,389 to 2,208 ms. Hot-subject online p99 fell from 8,389 to 198 ms. Both builds reported zero late or missing deliveries in this window. The other armed deliveries ended when their watcher closed.

## Churn

Both builds sustained the scheduled 2, 10 and 25 reconnect cycles/s. At 25 cycles/s, legacy used 25,518 Redis commands/s and current used 1,857. Current cut online p99 from 8,389 to 213 ms and offline p99 from 8,389 to 2,214 ms. Offline p50 went from 1,049 to 2,214 ms.

At that rate, 22,373 of 29,535 armed offline observations arrived on time on legacy, compared with 10,956 of 29,535 on current. The remaining 7,162 legacy and 18,579 current observations ended when the watcher closed. Both builds recorded zero late or missing deliveries. The offline grace and the timing of watcher disconnects matter when comparing these p99s.

In the current-only 100 activity changes/s profile, all 1,600 sockets were ready at the cutoff. All 105,176 armed offline and 105,176 armed online observations arrived on time, with p99 bounds of 2,222 and 220 ms.

## Gateway kill

Killing one gateway displaced 800 sockets on each build. Both delivered all 7,732 armed offline observations during the 150-second hold. Offline p99 was 94.941 seconds on legacy and 14.346 seconds on current. All 800 displaced sockets became ready on the surviving gateway. The driver restored them in 20.0 seconds on legacy and 17.0 seconds on current, including the reconnect ramp. Both delivered all 11,248 armed online observations, with p99 of 8,389 and 210 ms. The killed gateways restarted and returned to healthy.

A stage needs at least 99% of target sockets open and ready at the end and error frames across ramp and measurement to total at most 1% of the socket target. The legacy 4,000 stage failed both readiness and error thresholds. All 1,609 errors were `ROOM_LIMIT` frames. The driver stops at the first failed stage and records that result in JSON even when the process exits 0. Legacy separately passed the 3,500-socket quiet window. Current passed the highest configured stage with zero application frames during its quiet measurement and a mean gateway RSS of 1,663 MiB.

Probe cycles stayed assigned to their start phase. Legacy skipped 9 outage cycles and current skipped 19 because their actor sockets were unavailable. At the end of the last window, legacy had 1,580 of 1,581 intended sockets ready and current had 1,581 of 1,581. Current still recorded 13 missing offline deliveries in that window.

Both legacy gateways exited twice after the Redis kill at 00:36:32 UTC. The second current gateway exited once after its Redis kill at 00:43:05 UTC. Docker recorded exit code 2 and restarted every crashed gateway. Both builds logged the same stack trace. The two current panic traces came from a single process exit.

```text
panic: runtime error: invalid memory address or nil pointer dereference
messenger/ws/internal/redisroom.(*Listener).Close(...)
    messenger/ws/internal/redisroom/listener.go:108
messenger/ws/internal/room.(*Rooms).listen(...)
    messenger/ws/internal/room/room_subscription.go:34
```

On both revisions a failed subscription can return a nil `*Listener` through the room adapter as the `roomFeed` interface. The interface still holds a type, so the error path calls `Close` on the nil receiver. [Adapter](../../ws/internal/room/rooms.go#L78) · [Error path](../../ws/internal/room/room_subscription.go#L32)

Both clusters promoted a replica during the outage. In each stack `redis-2` rejoined as a replica and the runner's `CLUSTER FAILOVER` restored it as primary for slots 5461–10922. Final checks found both gateways healthy in each stack, six Redis nodes, all 16,384 slots assigned and `cluster_state:ok`. Both fault runs are included because the crashes reproduced the same product bug. The legacy smoke retry and one-socket probe were the only repeats.

Raw report JSON, exit logs and Docker events stayed on the benchmark host under `/tmp/presence-load/`.
